Surgical Dislocation of the Hip (SDH)

When bony deformity causing labral tears is excessive or highly complex (Perthes disease, OCD lesion), hip arthroscopy may not be able to address the entire problem adequately. In these circumstances, an open surgical procedure, called a surgical hip dislocation, is an option.

Treatment Approach

When to choose open surgery

Arthroscopy vs Surgical Dislocation

Hip Arthroscopy (Most Cases)

Many, if not most, hip injuries in young active patients including hip impingement, labral tears, labral insufficiency, loose bodies, hip instability, and snapping hip can be adequately treated with hip arthroscopy.

Outpatient · Same Day Discharge

Surgical Hip Dislocation (Complex Cases)

When bony deformity is excessive or highly complex, potentially resulting in residual deformity and higher risk for needing revision surgery, open surgery provides complete access.

Overnight Stay · 1-2 Days

Many, if not most, hip injuries in young active patients including hip impingement, labral tears, labral insufficiency, loose bodies, hip instability, and snapping hip can be adequately treated with hip arthroscopy when non-surgical treatments fail to improve symptoms and function.

Complex Conditions

Indications for surgical dislocation

Excessive Bony Deformity
Highly Complex Cam/Pincer
Perthes Disease
OCD Lesions
Cartilage Grafting (OATS)
Femoral Osteotomies
Labral Reconstructions
Revision Surgery Risk
Surgical Process

Safe dislocation technique

Surgical Hip Dislocation Process

1
Muscle-Sparing Access
Carefully dissect between muscles, avoiding cutting any muscles
2
Trochanter Osteotomy
Cut small part of thigh bone (greater trochanter) for access
3
Safe Dislocation
Dislocate hip joint while preserving blood supply to femoral head
4
Complete Access
Full access to entire acetabulum (socket) and femoral head (ball)
5
Complex Repairs
Perform pincer/cam resection, labral repairs, cartilage grafting
6
Osteotomy Repair
Repair osteotomy with metal screws at end of surgery

Difficult to reach areas using an arthroscope are easily accessible with the surgical hip dislocation when needed. Resection of excessive bone on the socket (pincer resection), abnormal, aspherical bone on the ball (cam deformity), and labral repairs are all performed easily with a surgical dislocation.

Post-Operative Recovery

Recovery timeline comparison

Recovery vs Hip Arthroscopy

Initial Recovery (0-6 weeks)

Slower than hip arthroscopy due to overnight stay, brace for 6-8 weeks, modified weight-bearing

Therapy Progress (7-8 weeks)

Becomes essentially the same as hip arthroscopy starting at 7-8 weeks

Strengthening (3 months)

Higher-demand strengthening and running begins at three months, same as arthroscopy

Return to Sport (5-9 months)

Full return-to-sport ranges from 5-9 months depending on sport, consistent with hip arthroscopy

While hip arthroscopy is an outpatient surgery, allowing patients to go home the same day, the surgical hip dislocation, as an open surgery, is more invasive and an overnight stay is generally recommended. Most patients are able to go home 1-2 days after surgery.

Weight-bearing (the amount of weight one is able to put on the operative leg) will be modified, and crutches will be provided and required after surgery for six weeks while the repair heals. Therapy will begin immediately to facilitate decreased swelling and pain, muscle activation and motion, and eventually improve strength to maximize function after surgery.

Expert surgical hip dislocation

If you have complex hip deformities or conditions that may not be adequately addressed with arthroscopy alone, surgical hip dislocation provides complete access for comprehensive repair while preserving hip function.
