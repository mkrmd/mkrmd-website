Avascular Necrosis (AVN)

Avascular necrosis (AVN), or osteonecrosis, is a painful hip condition in adults that results from a compromise or injury to the blood supply to the bone directly below the weight-bearing cartilage of the ball of the hip joint.

Disease Progression

Blood supply loss to bone collapse

AVN Progression Timeline

1
Blood Supply Loss
Injury to blood supply to the bone below weight-bearing cartilage
2
Bone Death
Without normal blood supply, bone undergoes progressive necrosis (dies)
3
Bone Weakening
Dead bone becomes weak and unable to support weight-bearing
4
Collapse
Weakened bone begins to collapse with continued weight-bearing

Over time, collapse leads to persistent pain, stiffness and progressive arthritis of the hip joint.

Risk Factors

Common causes of AVN

The cause of AVN is frequently related to a variety of environmental, nutritional or medical factors including:

Chronic Corticosteroid Use

Long-term steroid medication can disrupt blood supply

Prior Chemotherapy

Cancer treatments may damage bone blood vessels

Autoimmune Disease

Conditions affecting blood vessel function

Blood-Clotting Disorders

Conditions that affect blood flow to bone

Sickle-Cell Disease

Disorder or trait affecting blood circulation

Chronic Alcohol Use

Long-term alcohol consumption impacts bone health

Some cases have no identifiable cause.

Treatment Options

Early intervention vs joint replacement

Time-Sensitive Treatment

Early detection is critical for successful treatment. Once collapse occurs, the window for bone-preserving procedures closes.

If detected early, AVN can be treated with a surgery called a core decompression in an attempt to restore the blood supply to the bone.

When AVN has become too advanced and the ball (femoral head) collapses, osteoarthritis results. At that point, the best surgical option is a total hip replacement.

Expert treatment for avascular necrosis

If you have risk factors for AVN or are experiencing hip pain that could be related to blood supply problems, early evaluation is crucial to preserve your hip and avoid the need for joint replacement.
