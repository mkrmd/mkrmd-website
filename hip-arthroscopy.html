Hip Arthroscopy

Arthroscopy is a minimally-invasive surgical technique commonly performed on many joints including the shoulder, knee, elbow, hip, ankle and wrist by making small incisions around the joint to allow for insertion of a small camera.

Hip arthroscopy is performed by distracting the hip joint, separating the ball from the socket to allow for insertion of the camera and tools.

Minimally Invasive Advantages

Benefits of arthroscopic technique

Less Swelling

Smaller incisions result in reduced post-operative swelling and inflammation

Minimal Tissue Disruption

Less soft tissue dissection and muscle disruption compared to open surgery

Excellent Visualization

High-definition camera provides superior view inside the joint

Effective Treatment

Proven effective for treating most common hip injuries and conditions

Conditions Treated

Hip injuries treated arthroscopically

Hip Impingement (FAI)
Labral Tears
Cartilage Injuries
Loose Bodies
Snapping Hip
Hip Instability

Using 2-3 common portals in the front aspect of the hip, a variety of common hip injuries can be effectively treated. Hip arthroscopy generally takes 1-2 hours on average depending on the extent of the hip injury and bony deformity.

Surgical Procedures

Procedures performed arthroscopically

Available Arthroscopic Techniques

Labral Repair
Labral Reconstruction
Cam Resection
Pincer Resection
Capsule Closure/Tightening
Hip Flexor Lengthening

Hip arthroscopy does have technical limitations. When these limitations are present, other open surgeries are needed including pelvic and femoral osteotomies and surgical dislocation of the hip.

Recovery Process

Post-operative rehabilitation

Recovery Timeline

0

Surgery Day

Same-day procedure - no hospital stay required. Cold compression device recommended for pain and swelling reduction.

1

Next Day

Physical therapy begins the following business day with specialized hip rehabilitation protocol.

2-6

2-6 Weeks

Protected weight-bearing period to protect surgical repairs. Hip brace required except during PT and hygiene.

3

3 Months

Running program may begin if motion, function, and pain have improved appropriately.

6

6 Months

Most athletes feel ready to return to play at or near pre-surgery level, though some may take longer.
