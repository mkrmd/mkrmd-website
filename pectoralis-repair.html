Pectoralis Repair

A pectoralis major tendon rupture is an injury of the pectoralis major muscle in which the tendon typically tears off of the arm bone (humerus) or tears at the junction of the tendon and muscle. For a full rupture, surgical repair is the best treatment — reattaching the tendon to restore strength, function, and the natural contour of the chest.

For a complete tear, surgery is strongly preferred: studies consistently show that operative repair produces better outcomes than non-surgical treatment — in strength, function, and the overall appearance of the chest and shoulder after the injury.

During the procedure, the surgeon locates the ruptured end of the tendon and clears away any connective or scar tissue that has formed in response to the injury. The end of the tendon is then reinforced with several sutures, and its original attachment site on the humerus is prepared to promote the best possible healing.

Using specialized suture anchors and heavy-duty sutures, the tendon is then secured firmly back to its attachment point on the upper arm bone, restoring the connection between the chest muscle and the arm.

Recovery involves about six weeks in a sling to protect the repair, followed by several months of therapy to rebuild range of motion and strength. Athletes can generally expect to return to sport after four to six months, easing back into weightlifting with lighter weights and higher repetitions before progressing to heavier loads.

Repairing the torn pectoralis

A complete pectoralis tear repairs best when addressed promptly, so timely evaluation matters. A specialized assessment can confirm the rupture and determine whether surgical repair is the right path back to full strength and function.
