Core Muscle Injury

Core muscle injury, also known as athletic pubalgia or "sports hernia," is a common injury in athletes involving tears or strain to the muscles, tendons, and ligaments in the lower abdomen and groin area. This condition causes chronic groin pain and can significantly impact athletic performance.

Often misdiagnosed, core muscle injuries require specialized evaluation and treatment to restore full function and prevent chronic pain.
Core Anatomy

Understanding the core muscle complex

💪

Rectus Abdominis

Main abdominal muscle providing core stability and flexion strength

🔗

Conjoint Tendon

Junction where abdominal muscles attach to pubic bone - common injury site

⚡

Adductor Muscles

Inner thigh muscles that work with core for hip stability and movement

🏗️

Inguinal Ligament

Supporting structure connecting hip to lower abdomen

🔄

Pubic Symphysis

Joint where left and right pubic bones meet - central stability point

🛡️

Fascia & Ligaments

Connective tissues that bind muscles and provide structural support

The core muscle complex is a intricate network of muscles, tendons, and ligaments that work together to provide stability and power transfer between the upper and lower body. When these structures become weakened, torn, or imbalanced, it can result in significant groin pain and functional limitations.

Clinical Presentation

Signs and symptoms

⚡
Chronic groin pain
🏃
Pain with sprinting or cutting
🤧
Pain with coughing or sneezing
🛏️
Pain getting out of bed
🦵
Weakness with hip adduction
🔥
Burning sensation in groin
🎯
Point tenderness over pubic bone
Difficulty with explosive movements

Symptoms typically develop gradually and worsen with continued athletic activity. The pain is often described as a deep, aching sensation in the groin that can radiate to the inner thigh or lower abdomen. Unlike a traditional hernia, there is usually no visible bulge.

Sports that involve rapid acceleration, deceleration, cutting movements, and kicking place the highest demand on the core muscle complex. Athletes in these sports are particularly susceptible to core muscle injuries due to the repetitive stress and explosive movements required.

Diagnostic Evaluation

Proper diagnosis methods

Comprehensive Diagnostic Approach

Clinical Examination

Detailed physical assessment including palpation, strength testing, and specific provocative maneuvers to identify pain patterns

MRI Imaging

Specialized MRI sequences to visualize muscle tears, tendon injuries, and inflammation in the core muscle complex

Dynamic Ultrasound

Real-time imaging during muscle contraction to assess muscle function and identify areas of weakness

Diagnostic Injection

Targeted anesthetic injection to confirm the source of pain and rule out other conditions

Sports History

Detailed analysis of training patterns, mechanism of injury, and functional limitations

Differential Diagnosis

Rule out hip pathology, true inguinal hernia, nerve entrapment, and other causes of groin pain

Treatment Strategies

Conservative vs surgical treatment

Treatment Approach Options

Conservative Treatment

  • Rest from aggravating activities (4-6 weeks)
  • Targeted physical therapy and core strengthening
  • Anti-inflammatory medications
  • Corticosteroid or PRP injections
  • Manual therapy and soft tissue mobilization
  • Gradual return to sport protocol
Initial treatment approach for 3-6 months before considering surgery

Surgical Treatment

  • Core muscle repair (strengthening weak areas)
  • Adductor release (tension reduction)
  • Nerve decompression if indicated
  • Tissue reinforcement with mesh if needed
  • Minimally invasive techniques when possible
Recommended when conservative treatment fails after 3-6 months

The majority of core muscle injuries respond well to conservative treatment when caught early. However, chronic cases or those that fail conservative management may require surgical intervention to restore function and eliminate pain. The choice between treatments depends on the severity of injury, duration of symptoms, and the athlete's goals.

Expert core muscle injury diagnosis

If you're experiencing chronic groin pain that's affecting your athletic performance, proper evaluation and diagnosis is essential for effective treatment and return to sport.
