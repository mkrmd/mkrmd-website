About Dr. Ryan

A practice built around return to activity.

Every patient who walks into Dr. Ryan's office has something they are no longer able to do — a sport they've stepped back from, a trail they've stopped running, a child they can no longer lift, a career move they've put on hold.

The first job of the visit is to understand exactly what that activity is. The second is to design a diagnostic and treatment pathway that gets the patient back to it.

That framing — return to activity as the North Star, not just pain relief or a clean scan — shapes the entire practice. It determines which imaging gets ordered, how surgical decisions are made, and what the rehabilitation timeline looks like. It is also what elite athletes demand by default, and what Dr. Ryan believes every patient deserves.

Training That Shaped the Practice

American Sports Medicine Institute — Birmingham, Alabama
Dr. Ryan completed his orthopaedic sports medicine fellowship at the American Sports Medicine Institute, a world-renowned sports medicine mecca established by Dr. James Andrews, where the world's best athletes have sought care for decades. Training alongside five co-fellows in this environment — where Andrews and his predecessors have taught generations of the nation's best sports medicine surgeons — Dr. Ryan was exposed to cutting-edge sports medicine at the highest volume in the country.

During fellowship, he served as an associate team physician for the University of Alabama football team under head coach Nick Saban, contributing to the care of teams of national championship caliber. Working in that environment and delivering care at that level molded his ability to understand and deliver sports medicine and orthopaedic care at the highest possible standard — a foundation that led to his subsequent roles caring for Samford University, numerous high schools, an NBA G-League team, and eventually the Packers, along with three local colleges and area high schools.

The ASMI environment is unusual for how it forces fellows to think in parallel about tissue-level decisions and return-to-play timelines, about long-term joint preservation and short-term competition calendars. That dual perspective — biology and calendar — shapes how Dr. Ryan approaches every surgical plan today.

The same evidence, the same care, and the same return-to-activity mindset we apply on Lambeau Field, we apply in clinic every morning. That isn't marketing — it's the only way to practice.

Inselspital — Bern, Switzerland
Dr. Ryan subsequently completed a hip preservation fellowship at Inselspital in Bern, widely considered one of the founding centers of modern hip preservation surgery. The Bern experience added surgical hip dislocation, periacetabular osteotomy (PAO), and femoral osteotomies to a surgical toolbox that had previously been arthroscopy-dominant.

More importantly, this training — combined with his background in hip arthroscopy, open hip preservation, and total joint replacement — gives Dr. Ryan a unique perspective that counteracts the common nail-and-hammer, limited skill set of surgeons who perform only one of those approaches. The decision-making framework for when each approach is the right one is often the difference between a good outcome and an exceptional one.

The Packers

In 2025, Dr. Ryan was named Head Team Physician for the Green Bay Packers, joining a medical program built by Dr. Pat McKenzie that is widely regarded as one of the most thoughtful in professional sports. In that role, Dr. Ryan oversees the orthopaedic care of an NFL roster — the medical decisions, the surgical plans when they're required, and the return-to-play timelines that ultimately determine what happens on Sunday.

Beyond the Operating Room

A Colorado native, Dr. Ryan grew up playing ice hockey and football and was shaped by the family tradition of Notre Dame football — a background that, more than anything else, explains why he ended up in a profession built around the intersection of sport, medicine, and meticulous craft.

Outside of orthopaedics, his interests reflect his background as a former triathlete, marathoner, and endurance athlete: running, cycling, skiing, fly-fishing, and golf. He lives in the Green Bay area with his family.

Clinical Philosophy

Every patient deserves the standard of care an NFL player gets on Lambeau Field.

Sports medicine is often framed as a specialty for professional athletes — but in Dr. Ryan's practice, the majority of patients are not professionals. They are high school athletes trying to earn a scholarship, collegiate competitors trying to get back on the field, weekend warriors trying to stay active into their 50s and 60s, and active adults trying to avoid a replacement.

What unites them is a simple expectation: the same standard of care an NFL player gets on Lambeau Field should be available to every patient, every day. That is what the practice is built to deliver.
