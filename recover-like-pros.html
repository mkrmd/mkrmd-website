Recover Like the Pros

The same recovery protocols, equipment, and techniques used by NFL players and elite athletes are available to every patient. Here's how to optimize your healing and get back to peak performance faster.

Recovery Philosophy

Professional-level recovery for every patient.

Elite athletes don't heal faster because they're special — they heal faster because they have access to the best recovery protocols, equipment, and support systems available, though genetics certainly play a role.

As the Head Team Physician for the Green Bay Packers, Dr. Ryan ensures athletes are directed to the right recovery protocols and monitors that they are recovering as expected based on their time away from injury or surgery. The detailed protocols are overseen by our head physical therapist, but the same evidence-based approaches — from advanced modalities to targeted nutrition strategies — are what every patient deserves access to.

This resource guide contains the specific protocols, equipment recommendations, and recovery strategies used at the highest levels of professional sports, adapted for patients recovering from orthopaedic surgery.

Detailed Protocols

Phase-Based Rehabilitation

Elite athletes follow structured rehabilitation phases designed to optimize tissue healing while progressively restoring function. Each phase has specific goals and objective criteria for advancement.

Phase 1: Protection & Motion (Weeks 1-6)

  • Protect surgical repair while allowing safe motion
  • Control pain and swelling using compression and elevation
  • Begin gentle range-of-motion exercises within protective limits
  • Maintain cardiovascular fitness with approved activities

Phase 2: Strengthening & Progression (Weeks 6-12)

  • Progressive strengthening targeting specific muscle groups
  • Restore full range of motion in all planes
  • Begin proprioceptive and balance training
  • Introduce light functional movements

Phase 3: Return to Activity (Months 2-9)

  • Sport-specific movement patterns and drills
  • Plyometric training when appropriate
  • Objective testing: strength, power, movement quality
  • Psychological readiness assessment
Professional Equipment Used

Blood Flow Restriction (BFR) training, Grade IV laser therapy, shockwave therapy, electrical stimulation, AlterG anti-gravity treadmill, isokinetic testing equipment, force plates for movement analysis, and sport-specific training tools.

At-Home PT Support

MyHealthTrack (myhealthtrack.com) - At-home app to facilitate physical therapy exercises and track progress on days you don't see your therapist. Helps maintain consistency and provides guided recovery protocols.

GameReady Compression Therapy

The GameReady system combines therapeutic cold and intermittent pneumatic compression to control pain and swelling more effectively than traditional ice.

Protocol

  • Temperature: 50-55°F for optimal therapeutic effect
  • Pressure: 20-40 mmHg intermittent compression
  • Duration: 20-30 minutes, 3-4 times daily
  • Continue for first 7-14 days post-surgery
Home Alternative

Game Ready units can be rented for home use. Alternative: Pneumatic compression devices like NormaTec or simple compression garments combined with ice therapy.

Electrical Stimulation

Electrical muscle stimulation prevents muscle atrophy and maintains strength when normal exercise isn't possible.

NMES (Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation)

  • Frequency: 35-50 Hz for strengthening protocols
  • Duration: 15-20 minutes, targeting specific muscle groups
  • Intensity: Maximum tolerable contraction
  • Use during immobilization period to prevent atrophy

TENS (Pain Relief)

  • Frequency: 80-100 Hz for acute pain relief
  • Duration: Can be used continuously as needed
  • Placement: Around surgical site or referred pain areas
Recommended Devices

Compex Edge (professional grade), PowerDot (consumer-friendly), or clinic-grade stimulation units during therapy sessions.

Grade IV Laser Therapy

High-powered therapeutic laser that penetrates deep into tissues to reduce inflammation, accelerate healing, and provide pain relief through photobiomodulation.

Protocol

  • Power: 8-15 watts for deep tissue penetration
  • Wavelength: 810-980nm for optimal tissue absorption
  • Duration: 5-15 minutes depending on area size
  • Frequency: 3-5 treatments per week during acute phase
Professional Systems

High-powered therapeutic laser systems used in professional sports facilities. Home-use lasers are significantly lower power and less effective.

Shockwave Therapy

Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) uses acoustic waves to stimulate healing, break up scar tissue, and improve blood flow to treatment areas.

Protocol

  • Frequency: 1-2 treatments per week for 4-6 weeks
  • Intensity: Gradually increased based on tissue tolerance
  • Applications: Tendinopathies, bone healing, scar tissue
  • Combined with other modalities for enhanced effect
Professional Equipment

Radial and focused shockwave devices used in elite sports medicine facilities for optimal treatment outcomes.

Anti-Inflammatory Nutrition

Professional athletes use targeted nutrition strategies to reduce inflammation and accelerate tissue repair during recovery periods.

Core Anti-Inflammatory Foods

  • Omega-3 rich fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines) 2-3x per week
  • Leafy greens and colorful vegetables (6-8 servings daily)
  • Berries and tart cherries for antioxidants
  • Nuts and seeds for healthy fats and protein

Protein Optimization

  • Target: 1.6-2.0g protein per kg body weight daily
  • Distribute evenly across meals (20-30g per meal)
  • Include complete proteins: lean meats, eggs, dairy, quinoa
  • Post-surgery: Consider protein powder supplementation

Evidence-Based Supplements

  • Vitamin D3: 2000-4000 IU daily (test blood levels)
  • Omega-3 fatty acids: 2-3g EPA/DHA daily
  • Vitamin C: 1000-2000mg daily for collagen synthesis
Professional Standard

NFL teams use third-party tested supplements (NSF Certified for Sport) to ensure purity and avoid banned substances. Brands include Thorne, Pure Encapsulations, and Designs for Health.

Sleep Optimization for Healing

Elite athletes treat sleep as a performance tool. Growth hormone release and tissue repair are maximized during deep sleep phases.

Sleep Hygiene Protocol

  • Target 8-9 hours per night during recovery periods
  • Consistent bedtime and wake time (within 30 minutes)
  • Room temperature 65-68°F for optimal sleep quality
  • Complete darkness or blackout curtains

Pre-Sleep Routine

  • No screens 1 hour before bed (blue light disrupts melatonin)
  • Gentle stretching or meditation for 10-15 minutes
  • Light protein snack if hungry (Greek yogurt, cottage cheese)
  • Keep bedroom for sleep only (no TV or work)

Pain Management for Sleep

  • Elevate surgical site if recommended
  • Use supportive pillows for positioning
  • Consider white noise to mask disruptions
  • Ice or cold therapy 30 minutes before bed if helpful
Sleep Technology

Professional teams use sleep tracking devices (WHOOP, Oura Ring) to monitor sleep quality and recovery metrics. Temperature-regulating mattresses and weighted blankets can also improve sleep quality.

Multimodal Pain Control

Professional sports medicine uses comprehensive pain management strategies that minimize opioid dependence while maintaining patient comfort and function.

Pharmacologic Approach

  • Regional anesthesia (nerve blocks) for initial pain control
  • Anti-inflammatory medications on schedule (not as-needed)
  • Acetaminophen around-the-clock for baseline analgesia
  • Short-acting opioids only for breakthrough pain

Non-Pharmacologic Strategies

  • Ice therapy: 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours when awake
  • Elevation above heart level when possible
  • Gentle movement within prescribed limits
  • Relaxation techniques: deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation

Activity Pacing

  • Break activities into smaller, manageable segments
  • Alternate between activity and rest periods
  • Listen to your body: increase activity gradually
  • Maintain gentle movement even on difficult days
Advanced Modalities

Professional facilities use cryotherapy chambers, contrast therapy (hot/cold), and therapeutic laser for pain control. Home alternatives include contrast showers and portable TENS units.

Psychology of Recovery

Elite athletes use mental performance techniques to maintain motivation, manage setbacks, and visualize successful outcomes during rehabilitation.

Goal Setting & Visualization

  • Set specific, measurable, achievable goals for each week
  • Visualize successful completion of rehabilitation milestones
  • Picture yourself returning to your favorite activities
  • Celebrate small wins and progress markers

Stress Management

  • Deep breathing exercises: 4-7-8 technique for relaxation
  • Mindfulness meditation: 10-15 minutes daily
  • Progressive muscle relaxation before sleep
  • Maintain social connections and support systems

Confidence Building

  • Keep a recovery journal tracking daily progress
  • Focus on what you can control (diet, sleep, therapy compliance)
  • Work with physical therapist to build movement confidence
  • Gradual exposure to activities that initially create anxiety
Mental Performance Tools

Professional teams use sports psychologists, meditation apps (Calm, Headspace), and biofeedback devices to optimize mental performance during recovery.
