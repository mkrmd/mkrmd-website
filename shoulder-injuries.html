Shoulder Injuries

The shoulder trades stability for mobility, leaving it prone to instability and to tendon and labral injuries. Explore the conditions Dr. Ryan treats — from rotator cuff and labral tears to instability, fractures, and arthritis.

Shoulder Injuries

Select any condition to learn more about its symptoms, causes, and treatment options.

Anterior Shoulder Instability

Recurrent forward dislocations or subluxations, often with an associated labral (Bankart) tear and potential bone loss.

Posterior Shoulder Instability

A less common instability seen in athletes who load the shoulder in flexion and internal rotation, such as with bench press or blocking.

Multidirectional Instability

Excessive looseness in more than one direction, often tied to ligamentous laxity and treated with capsular tightening.

Rotator Cuff Tears

Partial- or full-thickness tears of the cuff tendons, from small traumatic tears to massive chronic tears, causing pain and overhead weakness.

SLAP Tears

Superior labral tears running front to back, common in throwing and overhead athletes and marked by deep aching and loss of velocity.

Shoulder Impingement

Inflammation of the rotator cuff tendons and surrounding tissue, producing pain when raising the arm.

Biceps Tendon Injuries

Irritation or tearing of the biceps tendon at the shoulder, causing pain and limited motion — often alongside a labral tear.

AC Joint Separation

Injury to the acromioclavicular joint, ranging from a mild sprain to a complete separation with visible deformity.

Clavicle Fracture

A break of the collarbone — one of the most commonly fractured bones — causing sharp pain at the front of the shoulder.

Pectoralis Rupture

A tear of the pectoralis tendon, typically during heavy pressing, causing weakness and bruising at the front of the shoulder.

Frozen Shoulder

Progressive stiffness and pain from tightening of the shoulder capsule, with a gradual loss of both active and passive motion.

Shoulder Arthritis

Loss of the cartilage surfaces of the joint, leading to painful bone-on-bone contact and progressive stiffness.
