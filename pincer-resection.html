Pincer Resection

The hip joint is a ball-and-socket joint, and a normal hip has a perfectly round ball (femoral head) and a socket (acetabulum) that matches it and is concentric. Any bony abnormalities of the ball or socket can lead to bony impingement and injury to the cartilage and labrum.

Another form of hip impingement is pincer impingement, where the hip socket is too deep and covers too much of the ball.

Socket Anatomy

Normal vs excessive coverage

Hip Socket Coverage Comparison

Normal Hip Socket

Appropriate depth and coverage allowing normal range of motion without impingement

Pincer Impingement

Hip socket too deep and covers too much of the ball, causing impingement and cartilage damage

Combined Procedure

Frequently, the labrum is torn in the setting of pincer impingement, and when it is a labral repair is performed during the same surgery.

Surgical Process

Arthroscopic pincer resection

Pincer Resection Procedure

1
Arthroscopic Access
Enter hip joint through minimally invasive portals
2
Pincer Identification
Locate the excess socket rim causing impingement
3
Rim Resection
Remove excess bone using motorized burr
4
X-ray Verification
Ensure proper bone removal with portable x-ray
5
Socket Restoration
Reestablish normal socket depth and coverage
6
Labral Repair
Repair any labral tear if present

This excess coverage causes impingement and damage to the cartilage and labrum. If non-surgical treatments fail to alleviate pain and improve function, pincer impingement can be properly treated with surgery, including hip arthroscopy for the vast majority of cases, and occasionally a surgical dislocation of the hip.

A portable x-ray machine is used to ensure the proper amount of bone is removed and to help reestablish a socket with normal depth and coverage. Once adequately resected, the hip no longer impinges during motion and activity.

Rehabilitation from a pincer resection and labral repair typically follows the same outline in the hip arthroscopy section and is modified as needed.

Expert pincer resection

If you have pincer-type hip impingement causing pain and limiting function, arthroscopic pincer resection can restore normal socket coverage and prevent further damage to cartilage and labrum.
