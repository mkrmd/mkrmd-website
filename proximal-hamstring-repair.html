Proximal Hamstring Repair

Most hamstring injuries occur while running or sprinting, and the strain or tear occurs in the muscle. Occasionally, the hamstring tendon can tear off its bony proximal (near) attachment to the pelvis at the ischium or "sit bone".

Hamstring Injury Types

Muscle strains vs tendon ruptures

Hamstring Injury Comparison

💚

Muscle Strains (Common)

Occur while running or sprinting in the muscle belly. This type of injury does not require surgery, but therapy is vital to ensuring maximal recovery.

🔴

Tendon Ruptures (Surgical)

Hamstring tendon tears off the "sit bone" (ischium). The tendon retracts and cannot heal properly, resulting in pain, weakness, and poor leg control.

Risk Profile

Who gets proximal hamstring tears

Risk Factors for Tendon Rupture

Age Factor

Frequently occurs in patients over 50 years of age

Water Skiing

Common activity when straight leg hyperflexes forward

Tennis

Sudden trunk flexion with straight leg position

Falls

Accidental hyperflexion causing tendon rupture

The tear is caused when the leg is straight and the trunk hyperflexes (forward), causing the tendon to rupture off of the bone. This type of tear generally requires surgery, as the tendon retracts (pulls away from the bone) and cannot heal properly on its own.

Classic Symptoms

  • Sharp pain at the moment of injury
  • Audible "pop" sound
  • Extensive bruising in back of thigh
  • Weakness with leg control
  • Pain at the sit bone (ischium)
Surgical Procedure

Endoscopic repair technique

Minimally-Invasive Repair Process

1
Small Incisions
Made around the ischium (sit bone)
2
Camera Insertion
Visualize torn tendon and bony attachment site
3
Anchor Placement
Specialized suture anchors placed into bone
4
Suture Passage
Sutures passed through hamstring tendon tissue
5
Tendon Repair
Sutures tied down, securing tendon to bone

All partial and many complete proximal hamstring tendon tears can be repaired using a minimally-invasive endoscopic approach. Occasionally, if the tear is chronic, scarred down, or significantly retracted an open approach may be needed.

Partial tears may improve with non-surgical treatment, including anti-inflammatory medications, therapy, activity modification, and injections (steroid or PRP). If symptoms fail to improve, surgery is recommended.

Post-Operative Care

Recovery and rehabilitation

Recovery Protocol

Outpatient Surgery

Go home the same day as surgery

6-Week Brace

Prevents certain motions and protects the repair

Modified Weight-Bearing

Based on severity of tear, crutches required

Immediate Therapy

Begins immediately for swelling, pain, and strength

Weight-bearing (the amount of weight one is able to put on the operative leg) is modified based on the severity of the tendon tear, and crutches will be provided and required after surgery. Therapy will begin immediately to facilitate decreased swelling and pain, muscle activation and motion, and eventually improve strength to maximize function after surgery.

Expert hamstring tendon repair

If you have a proximal hamstring tendon rupture causing pain, weakness, and functional limitations, minimally invasive endoscopic repair can restore strength and function to this critical muscle group.
