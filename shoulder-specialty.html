Shoulder Surgery & Instability

Arthroscopic stabilization, rotator cuff repair, and shoulder reconstruction — with a focus on restoring stability and function for athletes who depend on overhead throwing and contact sports.

Shoulder Surgery Philosophy

Stability restoration tailored to activity demands and contact exposure.

The shoulder trades stability for mobility — it's the most mobile joint in the body, but that mobility comes at the cost of inherent stability. When that balance is disrupted by injury, surgery must restore both.

Dr. Ryan's approach to shoulder surgery is built around understanding each patient's specific activity demands. A quarterback needs different stability than a swimmer, and a collision sport athlete has different risk tolerance than a golfer. This means tailoring surgical technique, rehabilitation protocols, and return-to-play criteria to the specific demands of each patient's sport and position.

Activity-Specific Surgery

Surgical technique is tailored to specific sport demands — contact athletes may require more aggressive stabilization than overhead athletes to prevent recurrent instability.

Latarjet for High-Risk Athletes

Collision sport athletes and those with significant bone loss benefit from bone block procedures that provide mechanical stability rather than soft tissue alone.

Throwing Athlete Considerations

Overhead athletes require surgical techniques that restore stability without over-constraining the shoulder and limiting the external rotation needed for throwing.

Revision Expertise

Failed instability repairs often require bone procedures or revision techniques that address the underlying pathology causing the initial failure.

Conditions & Procedures

From first-time dislocations to complex revision cases, Dr. Ryan offers comprehensive surgical management of shoulder instability and rotator cuff pathology.

Anterior Shoulder Instability

Recurrent anterior dislocations or subluxations, often with associated labral tears (Bankart lesions) and potential bone loss.

  • Recurrent shoulder dislocations
  • Fear of dislocation with activity
  • Pain with overhead activities
  • Apprehension with external rotation

Rotator Cuff Tears

Full or partial thickness tears of the rotator cuff tendons, ranging from small traumatic tears to massive chronic tears.

  • Shoulder pain at rest and with activity
  • Weakness with overhead lifting
  • Night pain disrupting sleep
  • Progressive loss of motion

Posterior Shoulder Instability

Less common form of instability typically seen in athletes who load the shoulder in flexion and internal rotation.

  • Shoulder pain with bench press
  • Posterior shoulder discomfort
  • Weakness in internal rotation
  • Sense of shoulder slipping

Multidirectional Instability

Excessive translation in multiple directions, often associated with ligamentous laxity and requiring capsular tightening procedures.

  • Shoulder subluxation in multiple directions
  • Generalized shoulder looseness
  • Difficulty with overhead activities
  • Associated neck and upper back pain

SLAP Tears

Superior labral tears from anterior to posterior, often seen in throwing athletes and overhead sport participants.

  • Deep shoulder aching
  • Pain with overhead throwing
  • Mechanical catching or clicking
  • Loss of throwing velocity

AC Joint Separation

Injury to the acromioclavicular joint ranging from mild sprains to complete separations requiring surgical reconstruction.

  • Point tenderness over AC joint
  • Pain with cross-body movement
  • Visible deformity in severe cases
  • Weakness with overhead lifting

Arthroscopic Bankart Repair

Minimally invasive repair of anterior labral tears using suture anchors to restore the labral bumper and capsular tension.

  • All-arthroscopic technique
  • Multiple anchor repair as needed
  • Capsular tensioning for stability
  • Return to contact sports 4-6 months

Latarjet Procedure

Bone block procedure transferring the coracoid process to the glenoid rim for mechanical stability in high-risk collision athletes.

  • Immediate mechanical stability
  • Ideal for collision sport athletes
  • Addresses significant bone loss
  • Open surgical technique

Rotator Cuff Repair

Arthroscopic repair of full-thickness rotator cuff tears using modern techniques to maximize healing potential and restore function.

  • Double-row repair technique
  • Biologic augmentation when indicated
  • Minimal-access arthroscopic approach
  • Progressive rehabilitation protocol

Capsular Shift

Surgical tightening of the shoulder capsule for multidirectional instability, restoring normal capsular volume and tension.

  • Open or arthroscopic approach
  • 360-degree capsular tightening
  • Customized to patient's laxity pattern
  • Extended immobilization required

SLAP Repair

Repair of superior labral tears in appropriate candidates, particularly throwing athletes under 30 with acute traumatic tears.

  • Selective repair in appropriate candidates
  • Consider debridement in older athletes
  • Biceps tenodesis alternative when indicated
  • Extended return to throwing timeline

AC Joint Reconstruction

Anatomic reconstruction of high-grade AC separations using modern techniques to restore both horizontal and vertical stability.

  • Anatomic coracoclavicular reconstruction
  • Preserves native anatomy when possible
  • Multiple graft and fixation options
  • Activity modification during healing

What to Expect

From initial injury through return to overhead sports, here's your pathway back to full shoulder function.

Initial Visit

Diagnosis & Imaging

Comprehensive examination including instability testing, strength assessment, and imaging review. MR arthrogram often needed to assess labral pathology and bone loss measurements.

Surgery Day

Arthroscopic Procedure

Most shoulder procedures are outpatient arthroscopic surgery. Instability repairs typically take 60-90 minutes. Latarjet procedures require open technique with 1-2 hour surgical time.

Weeks 1-6

Immobilization & Protection

Sling immobilization for 4-6 weeks depending on procedure. Early range of motion exercises begin within first week for rotator cuff repairs, later for instability procedures to allow healing.

Months 2-4

Motion & Strengthening

Progressive restoration of range of motion followed by strengthening. Overhead activities and external rotation are advanced cautiously to protect the repair while restoring function.

Months 4-6

Sport-Specific Training

Gradual return to throwing or overhead activities with progressive loading. Interval throwing programs for baseball players, progressive overhead loading for other athletes.

Months 6-12

Return to Competition

Return to full competition varies by procedure and sport. Contact sports typically 4-6 months, overhead throwing sports 6-12 months depending on position and level of play.
